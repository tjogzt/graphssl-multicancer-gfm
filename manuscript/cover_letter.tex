\noindent\textbf{Re: submission of an Article --- ``Self-Supervised Pretraining on Multi-Cancer Molecular Networks: Task Design Principles and the Role of Protein Language Model Features''}

\medskip
Dear Editors of Cell Reports Methods,

We submit the enclosed manuscript for consideration as an Article in \textit{Cell Reports Methods} (Methods Track). The work is authored by Qingqing Mo, Pingbo Chen, Cheng Xu, Ya Wang, Ting Hu, Qian Sun, and Tao Zhu.

\textbf{Headline findings.} We report three evidence-based results that delimit where graph self-supervised learning (SSL) adds value on top of fixed ESM-2 protein language model (PLM) features across multi-cancer molecular networks. (i) The reconstruction-first objective (NAMR) is the sole seed-stable encoder-quality driver, yielding held-out PPI link-prediction AUC 0.842 $\pm$ 0.016 across five equal-budget seeds; both CSP and the C3L contrastive loss underperform by margins of 0.021 and 0.046 respectively. (ii) PLM feature information content is necessary but not sufficient: replacing ESM-2 with random initialisation collapses NAMR performance to the 0.662 structural-baseline level, and the C3L contrastive objective never detaches from that baseline under any feature regime --- formally ruled out via 17 equal-FLOP ablation arms. (iii) CSP$+$NAMR joint pipelines are not universally reliable; they require a skip-decoder two-condition rejection filter, with one confirmed trigger case (\texttt{csp\_namr\_skipdiag\_s42.pt}) disclosed alongside the full 96-checkpoint registry.

\textbf{Why \textit{Cell Reports Methods} (Methods Track fit).} This submission maps cleanly onto the journal's Methods Track scope and editorial values at three levels. (1) \textit{Task-design methodology for biomedical graph SSL.} Rather than proposing a single new architecture, we deliver three explicit, testable task-design Guidelines (two STRONG, one CONDITIONAL) plus a pre-specified Level-1/2/3 three-tier analysis hierarchy, all anchored to an equal-budget five-seed ablation protocol (seeds 42/7/123/21/99). Methods-readers value exactly this kind of protocol-level guidance, not yet another encoder with an unreported training budget. (2) \textit{Full reproducibility packaged as an R wrapper.} We ship a minimal R-language entry-point (\texttt{R/RunMultiCancerSSL.R}) and a 10-command shell recipe (\texttt{README.md} + \texttt{Makefile}) that regenerate every figure, table, and in-text number from raw data downloads (TCGA/UCSC Xena, STRING v12, GDSC2, GTEx, METABRIC via cBioPortal, ESM-2 weights), with an accompanying 96-row checkpoint provenance registry serialized as JSON. This directly satisfies the \textit{Cell Reports Methods} Methods Track criterion that end-to-end reproduction must be a single-click or 10-command proposition. (3) \textit{STAR Methods-ready reporting with a full Key Resources Table and explicitly documented boundary negatives.} We separate patient-level, drug-response, and gene-level scopes as declared Limitations rather than inflating headline claims, and we package the entire Methods section as STAR Methods with a 30-row Key Resources Table (Chemicals, Assays, 6 Deposited datasets, Cell lines, Oligos, 7 Software packages, and 2 Other categories) formatted to match the journal's exact template. Zero administrative IRB-dbGaP approvals are required for the secondary analysis of these publicly released, fully de-identified cohorts, per journal policy.
